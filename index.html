Pituitary — Growth Hormone

3 diseases
AcroAcromegaly
GH excess in adults

Definition

  • Excess GH due to pituitary adenoma (>95%); ectopic GHRH/GH rare

Aetiology

  • Pituitary adenoma >95%; ectopic GHRH/GH (pancreatic tumour)
  • MEN 1 (6%)

Clinical Picture

  • Coarse facial features; spade-like hands; ↑shoe size; large tongue
  • Prognathism; interdental spaces
  • Excessive sweating/oily skin (sweat gland hypertrophy)
  • ↑prolactin (1/3 → galactorrhoea)
  • Pituitary tumour features: headache, bitemporal hemianopia, hypopituitarism

Complications

  • Hypertension
  • Type 2 DM (>10%)
  • Cardiomyopathy
  • Colorectal cancer
  • Sleep apnoea

Management

  • Trans-sphenoidal surgery (1st line)
  • Medical if persistent: somatostatin analogues (octreotide, lanreotide)
  • GH receptor antagonists (pegvisomant)
  • Dopamine agonists (cabergoline)
  • Radiotherapy
GigGigantism
GH excess before epiphyseal closure

Definition

  • GH excess BEFORE epiphyseal closure → excessive linear growth

Aetiology

  • Pituitary adenoma secreting GH in children/adolescents
  • May be part of MEN 1

Clinical Picture

  • Tall stature (excessive height); large hands/feet
  • Macrocephaly
  • Mild acromegalic features may develop after puberty

Complications

  • Joint problems
  • Hypopituitarism
  • Visual field defects
  • Cardiomyopathy

Management

  • Trans-sphenoidal surgery
  • Somatostatin analogues
  • GH receptor antagonists
  • Radiotherapy
GHDGrowth Hormone Deficiency
Impaired growth in children

Definition

  • Insufficient GH production → impaired growth in children

Aetiology

  • Pituitary adenoma; pituitary surgery/radiotherapy
  • Cranial irradiation; birth trauma
  • Genetic (GH1 gene); idiopathic

Clinical Picture

  • Children: short stature; delayed bone age; ↑weight; delayed puberty; hypoglycaemia (infants)
  • Adults: fatigue; ↑body fat; ↓muscle mass; osteoporosis; ↓quality of life

Complications

  • Short stature (permanent if untreated)
  • Osteoporosis
  • Cardiovascular risk; ↑adiposity

Management

  • Recombinant GH (subcutaneous daily)
  • Monitor IGF-1 levels
  • Treat any underlying cause

Pituitary — Prolactin

2 diseases
PrlProlactinoma
Most common functioning pituitary adenoma

Definition

  • Most common functioning pituitary adenoma; excess prolactin secretion

Aetiology

  • Prolactin-secreting pituitary adenoma
  • Microadenoma (<10mm, more common in women); macroadenoma (>10mm, more common in men)
  • Dopamine is physiological inhibitor of prolactin

Clinical Picture

  • Women: amenorrhoea; galactorrhoea; infertility; oligomenorrhoea
  • Men: ↓libido; impotence; gynaecomastia; visual field defects (macroadenoma)
  • Both: headaches; bitemporal hemianopia

Complications

  • Infertility
  • Osteoporosis (long-standing)
  • Visual loss (macroadenoma compressing optic chiasm)

Management

  • Dopamine agonists 1st line (cabergoline > bromocriptine)
  • Trans-sphenoidal surgery if resistant
  • Radiotherapy rarely
  • Visual field monitoring
GalacGalactorrhoea
83 · Inappropriate milk production

Definition

  • Inappropriate milk production in non-lactating individuals due to prolactin action on breast tissue

Aetiology

  • Prolactinoma (most common pathological cause)
  • Pregnancy, oestrogens, primary hypothyroidism (TRH stimulates prolactin)
  • Stress/exercise
  • Drugs (metoclopramide, domperidone, phenothiazines, haloperidol, SSRIs, opioids)
  • PCOS, acromegaly

Clinical Picture

  • Women: amenorrhoea + galactorrhoea
  • Men: impotence, loss of libido, galactorrhoea
  • If prolactinoma: headaches, visual disturbance, bitemporal hemianopia

Complications

  • Infertility
  • Osteoporosis (from hypogonadism)

Management

  • Dopamine agonists (cabergoline, bromocriptine)
  • Trans-sphenoidal surgery if resistant or compressive symptoms

Pituitary — ADH Disorders

4 diseases
SIADHSIADH
Syndrome of Inappropriate ADH

Definition

  • Syndrome of Inappropriate ADH secretion → water retention → dilutional hyponatraemia

Aetiology

  • Lung cancer (SCLC most common)
  • Pneumonia; meningitis; brain tumours; head injury
  • Drugs: carbamazepine, SSRIs, cyclophosphamide, chlorpropamide
  • Post-surgery

Clinical Picture

  • Usually asymptomatic until Na+ <120
  • Confusion; seizures; coma; anorexia; nausea

Diagnosis

  • Hyponatraemia; low serum osmolality (<270)
  • HIGH urine osmolality (>100)
  • Urine Na+ >40
  • Normal/↑volume status (no oedema/dehydration)

Complications

  • Cerebral oedema
  • Seizures; coma; death

Management

  • Fluid restriction (1st line, <1L/day)
  • Treat underlying cause
  • Demeclocycline (ADH antagonist)
  • Tolvaptan (V2 receptor antagonist)
  • Hypertonic saline if severe/symptomatic (Na+ <120 or seizures)
  • DO NOT give normal saline (will worsen)
CDICranial Diabetes Insipidus
Lack of ADH from posterior pituitary

Definition

  • Lack of ADH from posterior pituitary → inability to concentrate urine

Aetiology

  • Head injury (most common)
  • Pituitary surgery; craniopharyngioma; brain tumours
  • Meningitis; Sheehan's syndrome; idiopathic

Clinical Picture

  • Polyuria (3-20L/day); polydipsia; nocturia
  • Thirst; dehydration if no access to water
  • Preference for cold drinks

Diagnosis

  • Water deprivation test: urine osmolality remains low
  • Urine osmolality rises >50% after desmopressin

Complications

  • Dehydration
  • Hypernatraemia
  • Seizures; renal damage

Management

  • Desmopressin (synthetic ADH, nasal/oral/IM)
  • Treat underlying cause
  • Adequate fluid access
NDINephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus
Kidneys resistant to ADH

Definition

  • Kidneys resistant to ADH → inability to concentrate urine despite normal/high ADH

Aetiology

  • Congenital: V2 receptor or aquaporin-2 mutation, X-linked
  • Acquired: lithium (most common drug cause), hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, chronic kidney disease

Clinical Picture

  • Polyuria; polydipsia; nocturia
  • Dehydration; hypernatraemia

Diagnosis

  • Water deprivation test: urine osmolality remains low
  • NO response to desmopressin (unlike cranial DI)

Complications

  • Hydronephrosis (from chronic polyuria)
  • Hypernatraemia; seizures

Management

  • Treat underlying cause; stop lithium if possible
  • High fluid intake
  • Thiazide diuretics (paradoxically reduce polyuria)
  • Low-salt diet; NSAIDs (prostaglandin inhibition)
PPPsychogenic Polydipsia
71 · Excessive water intake

Definition

  • Excessive fluid intake (up to 6L/day) due to psychiatric disorder; unlike DI, no nocturia or increased thirst at night

Aetiology

  • Primary psychiatric disorder (emotional disturbances)
  • Often associated with schizophrenia

Clinical Picture

  • Excessive fluid intake; polyuria
  • NO nocturia; NO increased thirst at night
  • Emotional disturbances; low starting plasma osmolality

Diagnosis

  • Water deprivation test: starting plasma osmolality LOW (vs high in DI)
  • Urine osmolality >400 after dehydration (vs <300 in DI)
  • Acute: can concentrate urine during test; chronic: impairs concentration (mimics partial central DI)
  • Does NOT respond to exogenous ADH (like nephrogenic DI); ADH levels LOW (vs HIGH in nephrogenic DI)

Complications

  • Water intoxication; hyponatraemia
  • Seizures; coma

Management

  • Treat underlying psychiatric condition
  • Fluid restriction
  • Behavioural therapy

Pituitary Tumours & Mass Effects

4 diseases
PAPituitary Adenoma
Most common sellar mass

Definition

  • Benign tumour of anterior pituitary; most common sellar mass

Aetiology

  • Microadenoma <10mm; macroadenoma >10mm
  • Functioning: prolactinoma most common, GH, ACTH, TSH
  • Non-functioning adenomas

Clinical Picture

  • Mass effect: headache; bitemporal hemianopia (compression of optic chiasm)
  • Hypopituitarism
  • Hyperprolactinaemia (stalk effect)
  • Cavernous sinus invasion (CN III/IV/VI palsy)

Diagnosis

    Complications

    • Visual loss
    • Hypopituitarism
    • Pituitary apoplexy
    • Hydrocephalus

    Management

    • Trans-sphenoidal surgery (1st line for macroadenoma)
    • Medical therapy for functioning adenomas
    • Radiotherapy
    • Hormone replacement for deficiencies
    CraniCraniopharyngioma
    Most common suprasellar tumour in children

    Definition

    • Benign tumour derived from Rathke's pouch; most common suprasellar tumour in children

    Aetiology

    • Embryological remnant of Rathke's pouch
    • Calcified; cystic + solid components

    Clinical Picture

    • Children: short stature; headaches; visual problems (bitemporal hemianopia)
    • DI (50%); hypopituitarism
    • Raised ICP (vomiting, papilloedema)

    Diagnosis

      Complications

      • Visual loss
      • Hypopituitarism
      • Diabetes insipidus
      • Obesity (hypothalamic damage)
      • Hydrocephalus; recurrence

      Management

      • Surgical excision
      • Radiotherapy for residual tumour
      • Hormone replacement
      • Careful monitoring for recurrence
      PApPituitary Apoplexy
      Medical emergency

      Definition

      • Medical emergency — haemorrhage or infarction into a pituitary adenoma

      Aetiology

      • Spontaneous haemorrhage into adenoma
      • Precipitated by: anticoagulation, surgery, pregnancy, head injury, dynamic pituitary testing

      Clinical Picture

      • Sudden severe headache (worst ever); vomiting
      • Visual loss (ophthalmoplegia, bitemporal hemianopia)
      • Reduced consciousness
      • Hypopituitarism (acute ACTH deficiency most dangerous)

      Diagnosis

      • Pituitary MRI (haemorrhage into adenoma)
      • High-dose IV hydrocortisone immediately before any other investigation

      Complications

      • Permanent visual loss
      • Hypopituitarism
      • Death (if untreated)

      Management

      • IV hydrocortisone IMMEDIATELY (100mg)
      • IV fluids
      • Urgent neurosurgery (trans-sphenoidal decompression)
      • Hormone replacement
      SheehanSheehan's Syndrome
      Postpartum pituitary infarction

      Definition

      • Postpartum pituitary infarction due to ischaemic necrosis of enlarged pituitary

      Aetiology

      • Severe postpartum haemorrhage → hypotension → pituitary ischaemia
      • Pituitary enlarges during pregnancy (↑demand)
      • Anterior pituitary more affected than posterior

      Clinical Picture

      • Failure to lactate (most common early sign)
      • Failure to resume menstruation
      • Fatigue; weight loss; hypothyroidism
      • Adrenal insufficiency; loss of axillary/pubic hair

      Diagnosis

        Complications

        • Chronic hypopituitarism
        • Adrenal crisis if untreated
        • Osteoporosis
        • Infertility

        Management

        • Hormone replacement (cortisol, thyroxine, sex steroids)
        • Consider fertility treatment
        • Patient education about steroid sick-day rules

        Thyroid – Hyperthyroidism

        8 diseases
        GravesGraves' Disease
        12 · Autoimmune hyperthyroidism

        Definition

        • Most common cause of hyperthyroidism; autoimmune; diffuse goitre

        Aetiology

        • Autoimmune; anti-TSH receptor antibodies (TSI, thyroid stimulating immunoglobulin)
        • F>M (5-10:1); 20-50 years
        • Associated with other autoimmune: pernicious anaemia, vitiligo, T1DM
        • Smoking — risk factor for ophthalmopathy

        Clinical Picture

        • Hyperthyroidism: weight loss, heat intolerance, tachycardia, palpitations, tremor, anxiety, diarrhoea
        • Thyroid: diffuse smooth goitre; thyroid bruit
        • Specific: thyroid eye disease (proptosis, lid lag, lid retraction); pretibial myxoedema; thyroid acropachy

        Complications

        • Atrial fibrillation
        • Thyroid storm
        • Heart failure
        • Osteoporosis
        • Graves' ophthalmopathy
        • Pregnancy complications (preterm, miscarriage)

        Management

        • Carbimazole (1st line; titration or block-replace)
        • Radioactive iodine (RAI) — definitive
        • Thyroidectomy (if RAI contraindicated, large goitre, cancer)
        • β-blockers (propranolol) for symptom control
        • Pregnancy: propylthiouracil (1st trimester), carbimazole (2nd/3rd)
        TMNGToxic Multinodular Goitre
        13 · Autonomous nodular hyperthyroidism

        Definition

        • Hyperthyroidism from autonomously functioning nodules within a multinodular goitre

        Aetiology

        • Long-standing multinodular goitre
        • Autonomous nodule(s) escape TSH control
        • Iodine deficiency areas
        • Older patients (>50); F>M

        Clinical Picture

        • Hyperthyroidism symptoms (often milder than Graves)
        • Nodular goitre (irregular, bosselated)
        • NO ophthalmopathy; NO pretibial myxoedema
        • May have compressive symptoms (dysphagia, dyspnoea, stridor)

        Complications

        • Atrial fibrillation
        • Cardiac failure
        • Osteoporosis
        • Tracheal compression
        • Retrosternal extension

        Management

        • Radioactive iodine (preferred)
        • Thyroidectomy (large goitre, compressive symptoms, suspicion of malignancy)
        • Antithyroid drugs (temporary pre-RAI)
        • β-blockers
        TAdToxic Adenoma
        14 · Solitary hyperfunctioning nodule

        Definition

        • Single autonomously functioning thyroid nodule producing excess thyroid hormone

        Aetiology

        • Benign follicular adenoma
        • Autonomous TSH-independent hormone production
        • Solitary hot nodule on isotope scan

        Clinical Picture

        • Hyperthyroidism (mild)
        • Solitary palpable thyroid nodule
        • NO ophthalmopathy
        • Suppressed TSH; may be asymptomatic initially

        Complications

        • Atrial fibrillation
        • Osteoporosis
        • Thyrotoxicosis

        Management

        • Radioactive iodine (single dose often curative)
        • Thyroidectomy
        • Percutaneous ethanol injection (alternative)
        • Antithyroid drugs pre-treatment
        TSThyroid Storm
        15 · Medical emergency

        Definition

        • Life-threatening exacerbation of hyperthyroidism; medical emergency

        Aetiology

        • Precipitated by: infection, surgery, DKA, pregnancy, RAI treatment, trauma, non-compliance with medication
        • Usually in untreated or undertreated Graves' disease

        Clinical Picture

        • Fever (>38.5°C)
        • Tachycardia / atrial fibrillation
        • Altered mental status (agitation, confusion, coma)
        • Vomiting, diarrhoea; dehydration
        • Heart failure; shock

        Complications

        • Heart failure
        • Cardiac arrest
        • Multi-organ failure
        • Death

        Management

        • ABC
        • Propranolol IV (rate control)
        • Carbimazole (blocks new hormone synthesis)
        • Lugol's iodine / KI — 1 hour AFTER carbimazole (Wolff-Chaikoff effect)
        • IV hydrocortisone (blocks T4→T3 conversion)
        • Cooling; treat precipitant; ICU
        TEDThyroid Eye Disease
        16 · Graves' ophthalmopathy

        Definition

        • Autoimmune inflammation of orbital tissues and extraocular muscles in Graves' disease

        Aetiology

        • Autoimmune; anti-TSH receptor antibodies on orbital fibroblasts
        • Glycosaminoglycan deposition → extraocular muscle enlargement
        • Smoking is MAJOR risk factor (3× worse)
        • More common in hyperthyroid Graves' but can occur in euthyroid/hypothyroid

        Clinical Picture

        • Proptosis (exophthalmos); periorbital oedema
        • Conjunctival injection
        • Diplopia (from extraocular muscle involvement)
        • Lid lag; lid retraction
        • Optic nerve compression (rare); corneal ulceration

        Complications

        • Optic neuropathy (urgent)
        • Corneal exposure /ulceration
        • Permanent double vision
        • Cosmetic disfigurement
        • Sight loss

        Management

        • Stop smoking!
        • Euthyroid state (antithyroid drugs)
        • Artificial tears
        • Selenium (mild disease)
        • Oral / IV steroids (moderate-severe)
        • Orbital radiotherapy; orbital decompression surgery (optic nerve compression)
        • TED usually active for ~3 years
        AmioAmiodarone-Induced Thyroid Dysfunction
        17 · Iodine-rich drug effect

        Definition

        • Thyroid dysfunction caused by amiodarone (iodine-rich drug, 37% iodine by weight)

        Aetiology

        • Type 1: iodine excess → Jod-Basedow effect → hyperthyroidism (in abnormal thyroid, e.g. MNG)
        • Type 2: destructive thyroiditis → release of preformed hormone → hyperthyroidism → then hypothyroidism (in normal thyroid)

        Clinical Picture

        • Type 1: features of hyperthyroidism; may have pre-existing goitre
        • Type 2: painful thyroid (sometimes); hyperthyroidism then hypothyroidism
        • Can also cause pure hypothyroidism (Wolff-Chaikoff effect)

        Complications

        • Worsening cardiac arrhythmias
        • Thyrotoxicosis in cardiac patients is dangerous
        • Hypothyroidism

        Management

        • Type 1: antithyroid drugs (carbimazole)
        • Type 2: oral prednisolone
        • Stop amiodarone if possible (often need to continue for cardiac reasons)
        • Thyroidectomy if severe/refractory
        • Total T4 may be misleading — use free T4/T3
        SCHyperSubclinical Hyperthyroidism
        72 · Low TSH, normal T4/T3

        Definition

        • Normal serum free T4 and T3 with TSH below normal range (usually <0.1 mU/L); often asymptomatic

        Aetiology

        • Multinodular goitre (particularly in elderly females)
        • Excessive thyroxine replacement; Graves' disease; toxic nodular goitre

        Clinical Picture

        • Often asymptomatic; mild hyperthyroid symptoms possible
        • Palpitations; weight loss; nervousness
        • TSH often reverts to normal → must be persistently low to warrant intervention

        Complications

        • Atrial fibrillation
        • Osteoporosis
        • May increase likelihood of dementia

        Management

        • Treat if persistently low TSH
        • Low-dose antithyroid agents for ~6 months (therapeutic trial)
        • Beta-blockers if symptomatic
        ThyrPrThyrotoxicosis in Pregnancy
        86 · Thyrotoxicosis during pregnancy

        Definition

        • Thyrotoxicosis occurring during pregnancy; TBG levels increase, raising total thyroxine but not free thyroxine

        Aetiology

        • Graves' disease (most common)
        • Transient gestational hyperthyroidism (HCG activates TSH receptor, resolves in 2nd/3rd trimester)

        Clinical Picture

        • Features of thyrotoxicosis
        • Untreated increases risk of foetal loss, maternal heart failure, premature labour

        Complications

        • Foetal loss
        • Maternal heart failure, premature labour
        • Foetal hypothyroidism

        Management

        • Propylthiouracil (PTU) in first trimester (carbimazole teratogenic)
        • Switch to carbimazole after 1st trimester
        • Keep free T4 in upper third of normal
        • Check TRAb at 30–36 weeks
        • NO block-and-replace
        • Radioiodine contraindicated

        Thyroid – Hypothyroidism & Thyroiditis

        12 diseases
        HypoHypothyroidism
        18 · Insufficient thyroid hormone

        Definition

        • Insufficient thyroid hormone production; most common cause: Hashimoto's thyroiditis

        Aetiology

        • Primary (>99%): Hashimoto's thyroiditis (most common UK), post-RAI, post-thyroidectomy, amiodarone, lithium, iodine deficiency (worldwide)
        • Secondary: pituitary/hypothalamic disease (rare)

        Clinical Picture

        • Tiredness; weight gain; cold intolerance; dry skin; coarse hair; constipation
        • Depression; menorrhagia; carpal tunnel syndrome
        • Periorbital oedema; slow-relaxing reflexes; bradycardia
        • Goitre (if Hashimoto's)
        • Myxoedema coma (severe)

        Complications

        • Myxoedema coma (medical emergency)
        • Hyperlipidaemia; cardiovascular disease
        • Pericardial effusion; megacolon
        • Infertility; cognitive impairment

        Management

        • Levothyroxine (oral, titrated to TSH); start 50-100mcg daily (lower if elderly/cardiac)
        • Check TSH every 6-8 weeks; target TSH 0.5-2.5
        • Pregnancy: pre-conception TSH <2.5
        • Myxoedema coma: IV liothyronine (T3) or IV levothyroxine + IV hydrocortisone
        HashiHashimoto's Thyroiditis
        19 · Most common cause of hypothyroidism

        Definition

        • Most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient areas; autoimmune

        Aetiology

        • Autoimmune destruction of thyroid
        • Anti-TPO antibodies (positive in >90%); anti-thyroglobulin antibodies
        • Associated: pernicious anaemia, T1DM, vitiligo, Addison's, SLE
        • F>M (8:1); genetic predisposition; HLA-DR5

        Clinical Picture

        • Firm diffuse goitre
        • Hypothyroidism features
        • May present transiently with hyperthyroidism (hashitoxicosis)
        • Often asymptomatic for years; gradually progressive

        Complications

        • Permanent hypothyroidism
        • Associated autoimmune conditions
        • Rare: B-cell lymphoma of thyroid (MALToma)

        Management

        • Levothyroxine replacement (if hypothyroid)
        • Annual thyroid function monitoring
        • Screen for associated autoimmune conditions
        PAHPrimary Atrophic Hypothyroidism
        85 · Autoimmune thyroid atrophy

        Definition

        • Autoimmune diffuse lymphocytic infiltration leading to thyroid atrophy; hypothyroidism WITHOUT goitre

        Aetiology

        • Autoimmune (Hashimoto's spectrum)
        • F>M (6:1)
        • Associated with T1DM, Addison's, pernicious anaemia

        Clinical Picture

        • Features of hypothyroidism (weight gain, lethargy, cold intolerance, dry skin, non-pitting oedema, constipation, menorrhagia, ↓reflexes, hoarse voice) but NO goitre (key distinguishing feature from Hashimoto's)

        Complications

        • Myxoedema coma
        • Cardiovascular effects, hyperlipidaemia

        Management

        • Levothyroxine replacement (start 25–50 mcg od, aim TSH 0.5–2.5 mU/L)
        HypoPrHypothyroidism in Pregnancy
        87 · Hypothyroidism during pregnancy

        Definition

        • Hypothyroidism during pregnancy; increased thyroid hormone demands

        Aetiology

        • Pre-existing hypothyroidism (Hashimoto's, primary atrophic)
        • Increased demands of pregnancy require higher thyroxine doses

        Clinical Picture

        • Features of hypothyroidism exacerbated by pregnancy

        Complications

        • Adverse pregnancy outcomes if untreated (miscarriage, pre-eclampsia, low birth weight)

        Management

        • Thyroxine safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding
        • Increase dose by 25–50 mcg as early as 4–6 weeks
        • Monitor TSH each trimester and 6–8 weeks post-partum; aim for low-normal TSH
        DeQSubacute (De Quervain's) Thyroiditis
